Quick Answer: When Should You Get Your Dryer Vent Cleaned?

If your dryer takes more than one cycle to dry clothes, the outside of the dryer feels extremely hot, or you notice a burning smell, your dryer vent is likely clogged with lint. Lint buildup in your dryer vent is the leading cause of residential dryer fires in the United States. Beyond the fire risk, a clogged vent makes your dryer work harder, take longer to dry clothes, and use more energy. Our professional vent cleaning service removes all lint, debris, and obstructions from the entire vent run - from the dryer connection to the exterior wall cap.

Common Dryer Vent Cleaning Problems We Fix

Fire Prevention

The U.S. Fire Administration reports that dryers cause an estimated 2,900 home fires each year, and the leading cause is failure to clean. Lint is highly flammable. A full vent cleaning dramatically reduces this risk for your household.

Dryer Taking Too Long

If your dryer takes two or more cycles to fully dry a normal load, restricted airflow is almost certainly the cause. A clogged vent forces the dryer to work with reduced exhaust capacity, trapping moisture inside the drum.

Clothes Hot to the Touch

Clothes coming out excessively hot at the end of a cycle mean the dryer is overheating due to poor ventilation. This damages fabrics and can trip safety thermostats, causing the dryer to shut down mid-cycle.

Musty Smell on Clothes

Moisture that can't escape through a clogged vent recirculates inside the drum, leaving clothes with a damp or musty odor even after a full drying cycle.

Exterior Vent Flap Not Opening

If the flap on your exterior vent cap doesn't open when the dryer runs, lint has blocked the vent line, the vent run has a crushed section, or the flap mechanism is stuck. We clear the entire run and verify proper airflow at the exit point.
